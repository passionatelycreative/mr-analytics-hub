Research guide / 01

A clear question.
A practical plan.

Give your analysis a purpose before you open the dataset. This short framework helps you define what to learn, what to measure, and how to judge the evidence.

Set the scopeChoose measuresCheck the dataDraw conclusions

Step 01

Connect the question to a decision

Write the problem in one sentence, name the people or process involved, and set a time period. Then state what you will do differently once you have an answer.

“Where are support requests taking longest to resolve this quarter, and which queues should we investigate first?”

List what is outside the study. A defined scope keeps unrelated observations from becoming the main story.

Step 02

Choose measures you can explain

  1. Identify the outcome. Define the quantity or category that directly answers the question.
  2. Specify the unit. State whether a row is a person, response, or event, and record the units for numeric columns.
  3. Choose relevant comparisons. Decide which time periods or groups you need and use consistent definitions.
  4. Set a comparison point. A previous period, stated target, or clear baseline helps make a result interpretable.

Step 03

Check what the dataset can support

  • Coverage: Does the dataset include the population and time period your question concerns?
  • Completeness: Which columns contain missing values, and could those gaps affect the result?
  • Consistency: Are categories, dates, and measurement units used consistently?
  • Unusual values: Are extreme values valid observations, recording errors, or signs of a different process?
  • Provenance: Can you explain where the records came from and how they were collected?

Step 04

Keep the conclusion close to the evidence

Summarize the main observation, show the relevant comparison, and state any limitations. Distinguish what you observed from possible explanations.

End with a practical next step: a follow-up question, a change to test, or additional data to collect. Revisit the original question before deciding the research is complete.
