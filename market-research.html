Understand your audience.
Question the assumptions.

Use customer feedback, surveys, and observational data to investigate a need, then check whether the evidence supports your interpretation.

Define the questionCollect evidenceCompare groupsReport findings

Step 01

Make the question specific

Choose an audience, an outcome, and a time period. Write down the decision you want the evidence to inform.

“Which onboarding steps do first-time users most often find difficult?”

A focused question helps you identify the feedback to collect and the categories you will need in your dataset.

Step 02

Collect evidence with context

  1. Define who is included. Record how participants were recruited and who might be missing from the sample.
  2. Use clear, neutral questions. Avoid leading language and questions that ask about two things at once.
  3. Keep collection methods consistent. Record dates, channels, response options, and how incomplete responses are handled.
  4. Remove identifying information you do not need. Use anonymous respondent identifiers when preparing a CSV for analysis.

Step 03

Compare like with like

Start by counting responses and checking missing values. Then compare relevant groups using the same definitions and time window.

  • Show both group sizes and percentages so small samples are visible.
  • Check whether category labels differ only in spelling or capitalization.
  • Look for differences in the way groups were recruited or surveyed.
  • Treat an observed association as a starting point for investigation; it does not establish a cause.

Step 04

Write a finding someone can use

State the question, describe your sample, and present the observation with its limits. Keep recommendations tied to the evidence and identify what you need to learn next.

“Among the people who responded, this step was mentioned most often. We should test a simpler version and check whether completion improves.”

Prepare your CSV

A useful starting structure

Use one row per respondent or observation. Clear column names could include:

  • respondent_id — an anonymous reference
  • response_date — when it was collected
  • audience_group — your relevant segment
  • rating — a defined numeric scale
  • feedback — an open-text response
